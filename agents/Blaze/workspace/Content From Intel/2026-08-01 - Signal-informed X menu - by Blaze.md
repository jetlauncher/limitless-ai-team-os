## 10 X/Twitter drafts

1. Terminal agents (Signal: Gemini CLI)
If your team only uses AI in chat, you're still asking humans to carry the workflow.

Put one repeatable task in the terminal today: research, draft, check, log.

The advantage is not the model.
It is where the model is allowed to work.

2. Model abundance (Signal: GPT-5.6 on Bedrock/Azure)
The next AI shift is not “smarter chat.”

It is cheaper intelligence inside normal tools.

Founders should stop asking, “Which model is best?”

Ask: “Which weekly decision deserves an AI check before my team acts?”

3. Context layer first (Signal: RAGFlow)
Most AI failures in companies are not prompt failures.

They are context failures.

If your SOPs, customer chats, proposals, and decisions live in people's heads, no model can save the workflow.

Build the memory layer first.

4. Real work is messy (Signal: ORCA-bench)
Agents are getting better at code.

But real business work looks like on-call: messy signals, late reports, unclear ownership.

Before you trust AI, give it logs, rules, and an escalation path.

No system, no delegation.

5. Private AI boundary (Signal: LocalAI)
Not every AI workflow belongs in the cloud.

Customer data, finance files, internal SOPs: some teams need local or private AI options.

The founder question is simple:

What can AI touch, and what must stay inside?

6. Company memory (Signal: Metis memory model)
The next wave of AI will remember more.

That sounds powerful until your company has bad memory.

Clean decisions, naming rules, meeting notes, version history.

AI memory only helps if business memory is not chaos.

7. From demo to workflow (Signal: Dify/Langflow)
No-code agent builders are not for playing with demos.

Use them to replace one recurring handoff: lead intake, quote prep, weekly reporting, customer follow-up.

One workflow. One owner. One metric.

8. Maker/checker/approver (Signal: multi-agent systems)
Multi-agent sounds advanced.

In practice, most companies need 3 roles first:

maker, checker, approver.

If you cannot define those for a human team, adding five AI agents will just make confusion faster.

9. Small-team systems (Signal: open-source agent stack)
Open-source AI tools are becoming good enough for serious operators.

That changes the cost curve.

Small teams can now build internal systems that used to need a vendor, if they can describe the workflow clearly.

10. 70% before review (Signal: AI research agents)
The market keeps asking when AI will do open-ended work.

I think founders should ask a smaller question:

What ambiguous task can AI already prepare 70% for my review?

That is where compounding starts.
