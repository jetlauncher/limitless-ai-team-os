### 1. Channel Title Still Shows "Jedi Trinupab" Instead of Brand-Aligned Name (HIGH PRIORITY)

**Observed:** The YouTube channel heading reads "Jedi Trinupab" — not "Jet Trinupab" and definitely not aligned with the Limitless Club brand position. For a premium founder-media brand, the displayed name should either be "Jet Trinupab" (correcting "Jedi") or carry the Limitless Club identity visibly alongside the personal name.

**Luxury cue violated:** Premium publications use clean, correct naming as foundational branding. The typo "Jedi" dilutes credibility for a high-value advisor to Thai founders and executives. At 122K subs, this signals carelessness at the brand's most visible label point.

**Quick implementation note:** Edit YouTube channel name field → change "Jedi Trinupab" to "Jet Trinupab". Optionally add subtitle "Limitless Club" if allowed (check YouTube's profile customization options).
**Priority: HIGH — fixes a credibility error at the top of the brand funnel.**

---

### 2. Thumbnail Visual System Is More Cohesive But Still Not Unified Now That Banner Is Updated (MEDIUM-HIGH PRIORITY)

**Observed:** Since the banner update, thumbnail palette is more polarized toward dark tones — but still inconsistent at thumbnail size:
- Several thumbnails use a **dark/graphite background with white text** (close to luxury direction). 
- Some thumbnails use a **blue/teal accent strip or graphic element** that clashes with the gold/bronze brand color system.
- At least one recent thumbnail has a **white/light base**, creating visual jarring when scrolling the channel page as a complete feed.
- No single unifying rule exists: should thumbnails be dark-with-accent, photo-first, or pure typography?

**Luxury cue violated:** Think of Bloomberg Businessweek or Monocle at thumbnail size — identical grid impression despite varied content. The blue accents and white-background thumbnails signal "YouTube creator" energy rather than "premium publication editor-in-chief."

**Quick implementation note:** Define a **one-rule thumbnail system**: (a) Always dark background (#17181A), (b) White or gold text only — no blues, greens, or reds, (c) One accent line in bronze (#94764A) top or bottom edge to anchor the Limitless Club identity. Apply this retroactively to the 5 most recent thumbnails and enforce on all future thumbnails.
**Priority: MEDIUM-HIGH — affects every scroll impression equally.**

### 3. Video Titles Mix Languages Without Editorial Discipline (MEDIUM PRIORITY)

**Observed:** The visible video titles alternate between English ("Complete Review of Manus AI," "Claude Fable 5 creates...") and Thai ("รีวิวค่า AI Agent ทั้งหมดที่ผมจ่าย..."). There's no visible pattern or rule governing when Thai vs. English is used — it seems ad hoc rather than editorially planned.

**Luxury cue violated:** Premium publications have a clear language positioning (e.g., FT English = English, Nikkei = Japanese). Mixed, unpatterned language at the channel level undermines brand recognition in scroll view where many titles are seen simultaneously. For Jet's Thai founder audience, pure Thai or a clear "Thai-first with English subtitles" system creates stronger identity than alternating titles.

**Quick implementation note:** Decide on **one positioning**: (a) All Thai titles with optional English subtitles in description/hashtags, OR (b) Consistent pattern like "Thai title + English subtitle format: Title | Subtitle." Pick one and apply to ALL videos in the thumbnail system. This also pairs with a naming convention doc for the content team.
**Priority: MEDIUM — affects brand recognition at volume.**

### 4. Channel Description/Link Section Lacks Premium Hierarchy (MEDIUM PRIORITY)

**Observed:** The description shows Thai text "เช็ครอบอบรมกับทีมผม Line OA 👉" plus raw link fragments. Multiple links appear via the "and 3 more links" button. The display treats all links equally — no visual priority given to the primary conversion point or brand statement.

**Luxury cue violated:** Premium editorial brands treat their channel description like a masthead: clear hierarchy (who → what → proof → CTA), clean language, and ONE primary action. The "and 3 more links" button signals clutter — like walking into a boutique that has five different entry doors instead of one grand entrance.

**Quick implementation note:** Consolidate the About/Description to 2-3 lines max: (1) Who Jet is in one line, (2) What Limitless Club does in one line, (3) ONE highlighted CTA link set as the primary (Line OA). Hide secondary links behind that CTA internally. Clean up Thai text for readability and remove emoji/arrow symbols from profile text. Remove any clutter from the link section to show maximum 1 visible row.
**Priority: MEDIUM — affects first-impression conversion quality.**

---

### 5. Subscribe Button Placement & Social Proof Is Underutilized (MEDIUM PRIORITY)

**Observed:** The "Subscribe" button is visible but has no design treatment relative to the premium brand identity. At 122K subscribers, this social proof point could be leveraged more aggressively in the banner/banner area — it's a signal of authority that should be positioned for maximum visibility above the fold.

**Luxury cue violated:** Premium publications and luxury brands always prominently display their authority signals (circulation numbers, subscriber counts, "As seen in" logos). The 122K number is right next to "Subscribe" in small gray text — a wasted credibility moment.

**Quick implementation note:** In the banner art, explicitly include "122K+ Founders" or "122K Thai Leaders Learn Here" as social proof near or integrated into the "Limitless Club" masthead area. This is the same principle: Vogue doesn't hide its readership number; it flaunts it.
**Priority: MEDIUM — authority signal should be at banner level, not buried.**
