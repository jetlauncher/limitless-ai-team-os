**Key Finding #1 — The "416,465 บาท!" Price Thumbnail is the Cheapest Signal.**
The orange gradient + massive golden price numeral (#3) reads like a Lazada/Shopee flash sale. This is the single most damaging luxury signal across Jet's entire YouTube ecosystem. No premium brand advertises with a giant price tag as a headline.

Video 1 uses electric blue, Video 2 uses cooler teal-blue, Video 3 uses warm orange-gold, Video 5 uses saturated yellow. That's four distinct color identities across one row. At first glance on mobile, a viewer can't tell these are all the same creator — they could be five different channels.

Despite color chaos, there are recurring layout bones: Jet portrait always present, right-third composition is becoming default, and headlines stack over Jet's torso. This is good scaffolding that just needs discipline.

## Micro-Upgrades (Ranked by Luxury Impact)

### Upgrade 1: Enforce "No Giant Price Numbers" Rule on All New Thumbnails
**Change:** Never let a price, dollar amount, baht figure, or percentage appear larger than 60% of the largest text in any thumbnail. If revenue/pricing is relevant, show it as small footnote text (e.g., "ผลลัพธ์: ฿416K/เดือน" at bottom-right corner, <30pt equivalent) — treated like a disclosure, not a headline.

**Why luxury:** Luxury brands never lead with price. Rolex does not put "฿350,000" on its billboard; it puts the watch. Large price numerals = discount retailer visual language. This one-rule elimination would immediately elevate the thumbnail row's average luxury score by 1–2 stars.

**Implementation:** Thumbnail checklist addendum rule #1. Flag any thumbnail with >60% size pricing text before render.

**Priority:** 🔴 HIGH — active on this week's orange gradient video thumbnail

---

### Upgrade 2: Unified Thumbnail Accent Color → Bronze-Exclusive Grid
**Change:** All new thumbnails use exactly ONE accent color: #94764A bronze (or its near-black variant #B8956A for light text highlights). No blue, no orange gradients, no raw screenshots as backgrounds, no yellow boxes. Dark background (black/near-black photo or #17181A solid) + Jet portrait on right third + one bronze-accented element max per thumbnail (e.g., the key word phrase is rendered in bronze).

**Why luxury:** Every premium visual identity has a signature palette — Chanel's black/white/gold, Hermès's orange, Rolex's green. Having five thumbnail colors makes Jet feel like a tech aggregator channel, not an authority figure at his tier. One color across 15 uploads looks intentional by design (like a magazine), random by default.

**Implementation:** Create a one-page "Thumbnail System Card" showing the bronze-accent system with 3 layout examples + 2 "bad examples." Reference it before every thumbnail render.

**Priority:** 🔴 HIGH — solves the multi-color identity problem at its root

---

### Upgrade 3: Remove Red Subscribe Button from Banner → Let Native Platform CTA Earn Authority
**Change:** Recreate channel banner without ANY overlay graphics for calls-to-action. Keep: Jet portrait (right third), crowd photo inset (optional, small scale, lower-left corner as texture not primary element), Thai mission text centered/left ("คนไทย 1,000,000 คนต้องเก่ง AI") in bold white/gold, Thin bronze divider line near bottom edge, @JEDITRINUPAB + Limitless Club signature after the divider. Remove: Red "SUBSCRIBE" button, all social media icons (use native YouTube profile link row instead), all CTA copy ("Subscribe" / "Follow" / etc.).

**Why luxury:** On a luxury storefront, you walk past a clean window display — the product IS the ad. The red subscribe button is the visual equivalent of a shopkeeper waving at passers-by from the door. Removing it signals: "My work speaks for itself." (Confidence = premium.) Also removes an urgent/commodity color from the channel's top-of-funnel real estate.

**Implementation:** Recreate banner in Canva/Figma at 2560×1440. Export as PNG ≤4MB. Upload via YouTube Studio → Customization → Branding. Test mobile crop zones and TV safe areas before publishing.

**Priority:** 🟡 MEDIUM — high visual impact but less frequently seen than thumbnails (banner is full-width header, not repeated grid)

### Upgrade 4: Standardize Thumbnail Layout Grid to "Three Zone Rule"
**Change:** Every thumbnail must follow this exact layout system:
- **Zone A (right 33%):** Jet's face or upper body portrait (consistent lighting/angle — always facing slightly left toward center, warm studio lighting)
- **Zone B (left-center 50%):** One element max — either a product/mockup screenshot OR one-word/short-headline in bronze accent + supporting Thai text in white on dark BG
- **Zone C (bottom-left corner):** Tiny Limitless Club mountain logo (10px scale, ~3% opacity) as watermark — consistent anchor across the grid

**Why luxury:** Editorial publication grids work because every frame belongs to the same system. The Daily Telegraph, Financial Times, or Vogue thumbnails all follow strict compositional rules — they look different but feel identical. Three zones create instant brand recognition without repeating an exact design template (which feels cookie-cutter).

**Implementation:** Create a Canva template with guide lines at 33% and 83% vertical positions. Every thumbnail render snaps to these guides.

**Priority:** 🟡 MEDIUM — foundational but requires retraining the render process; compounding effect over time

---

### Upgrade 5: Title-Thumbnail Alignment Protocol
**Change:** When a video title is feature-first/utility ("AI จีนตัวนี้ทำเว็บ ..."), pair it with a thumbnail in **full restraint mode**: jet portrait + one-line bronze headline, zero product screenshots, zero stacked logos. When visual complexity IS needed (product/tool showcase), the title shifts to an **authority frame** ("Claude vs ChatGPT: ฝั่งไหนชนะปี 2026") rather than a feature list. Titles and thumbnails should amplify each other, not double down on "feature stacking."

**Why luxury:** Premium communication uses contrast between headline and visual — the text gives context; the image gives emotion/mood. When both say "look at all these features," compression kills impact. Authority headlines with restraint visuals = financial Times/WSJ editorial balance. Feature headlines + feature collages = tech blog aggregator. Subtle but real luxury differential.

**Implementation:** Pre-publish checklist question: "Does the thumbnail add something the title doesn't already say?" If no → simplify to portrait + one accent word.

**Priority:** 🟡 MEDIUM — internal process change, zero visual asset work needed from Jet or designer
