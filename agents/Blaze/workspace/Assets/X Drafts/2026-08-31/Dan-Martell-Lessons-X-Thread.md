### 1/10

I invested more than $50,000 to learn from my coach, Dan Martell.

8 lessons I’m still applying:

### 2/10 — Proximity expands what you believe is possible
2/

You cannot build a bigger future while taking all your cues from your current environment.

Getting close to people operating at the next level does more than inspire you.

It gives you a new reference point for what “normal” can look like.

### 3/10 — Simplify before you scale
3/

Dan showed me first-principles thinking in action:

Create content for the customer you want to attract.
Start the conversation.
Make the offer.
Remove everything that does not create value.

Complexity often makes us feel sophisticated while slowing the business down.

### 4/10 — Letting go is a leadership responsibility
4/

If I keep doing work someone on my team could own, I am not being humble.

I am denying them the opportunity to grow—and keeping myself away from the work only I can do.

The next level usually requires releasing an old identity, task, or approval process.

### 5/10 — Increase the value of the hour, not the number of hours
5/

Dan breaks leverage into four forms:

- Code
- Capital
- Content
- Collaboration

A bigger company should not require the founder to work infinitely more hours.

It should make each hour create more value.

### 6/10 — Treat your calendar like a promise to yourself
6/

Many people honor commitments to everyone except themselves.

Dan’s discipline is simple: if something is important enough to enter the calendar, it gets done.

Doing the right work during the week is what protects your nights, weekends, health, and family time.

### 7/10 — Systems remove low-cost anxiety
7/

Dan applies the software principle DRY—“Don’t Repeat Yourself”—to business.

Design the process.
Document it.
Follow it.
Inspect it.
Improve it once.

A checklist is not bureaucracy when it prevents the same mistake—and the same stress—from returning.

### 8/10 — Study ideas until they become your own
8/

Dan doesn’t just read books.

He studies them, rewrites the ideas that move him in his own language, and finds one useful insight to share with the world.

Consumption is not learning.

Learning is what changes how you see, decide, and act.

### 9/10 — Be selective about who gets the “power of opinion”
9/

One of Dan’s most personal lessons to me:

Choose a small group of people whose opinions are allowed to shape your decisions.

Take advice from people who have achieved the specific result you want—not from every spectator with an opinion.

### 10/10 — Value your time, then make bigger plans
10/

Dan told me I was underestimating my value—and listening to the wrong opinions.

His challenge:

Value your time.
Build the team.
Be more fully yourself.
Make uncomfortable plans.

$50,000 was expensive.

Staying the same would have cost more.
