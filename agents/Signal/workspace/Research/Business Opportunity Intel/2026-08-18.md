**Scan window:** 2026-08-17 05:31 → 2026-08-18 05:31 Asia/Bangkok  
**Selection rule:** Underlying change had to occur inside the window; maximum three signals; commercial consequence over novelty.

## 1) Managed Hermes/OpenClaw is now a commercial product category

- **Verified source/date:** DigitalOcean’s official investor release, **2026-08-17**; Google News RSS and syndicated copies timestamped it at **12:00 UTC / 19:00 Bangkok**.[2][3]
- **What changed — fact:** Cloudways launched **Managed AI Agents** in general availability with **OpenClaw and Hermes**.[2]
- **Why it matters to Jet — interpretation:** This is direct market validation for the operating model Jet already teaches and runs. As hosting becomes commoditized, the premium value moves to workflow design, permissions, source-of-truth connections, evaluation, recovery, and ongoing governance—not “installing an agent.”
- **Revenue/offer action:** Add **Runtime Ownership & Portability** to the existing AI Leverage Audit: who controls hosting, data, credentials, backups, observability, exit/migration, and incident response. Use failed controls as a remediation upsell; do not create another generic managed-agent offer while P2 remains Proposed.

## 2) AWS gives OpenClaw agents bounded payment capability

- **Verified source/date:** AWS Machine Learning Blog, **2026-08-17 08:19:56 PT / 23:19:56 Bangkok**.[1]
- **What changed — fact:** AWS published an OpenClaw integration with Amazon Bedrock AgentCore payments and x402. The `aws-agents-pay` pattern supports bounded, human-approved **testnet** payments for APIs, MCP servers, and web content, with recipient, asset, network, per-payment, cumulative-budget, and expiry controls.[1]
- **Why it matters to Jet — interpretation:** Agent spending is moving from a hypothetical risk to a measurable control surface. Any client allowing agents to buy tools/data will need authorization limits, wallet isolation, approvals, logs, and reconciliation.
- **Revenue/offer action:** Add an **Agent Spending Controls** test to the AI Leverage Audit: recipient allowlist, per-action and session budget, credential separation, expiry, human approval threshold, receipt/proof capture, and rollback/reconciliation. Sell remediation only after a controlled test; do not present the testnet demo as production-ready autonomy.

## Best immediate move

**Upgrade the existing AI Leverage Audit, not the product stack:** add the two scored checks above—**Runtime Ownership & Portability** and **Agent Spending Controls**. Together they create a defensible first-party angle: “Managed agents make deployment easier; money-moving agents make governance mandatory.”
