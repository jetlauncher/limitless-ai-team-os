## Signal — AI workflow economics now require a latency tier, not only cost and quality

### What changed — fact
- On August 13, OpenAI introduced a preview **Ultrafast** API service tier for GPT-5.6 Sol, reporting up to **14× faster** generation and up to **750 output tokens/second**, powered by Cerebras.[1]
- Also on August 13, GitHub began rolling out **Gemini 3.7 Flash** across Copilot surfaces including the cloud agent, CLI, app, IDEs, and coding agent. GitHub lists provider-based pricing of **$0.75/M input**, **$0.075/M cached input**, and **$3.75/M output tokens**.[2][3]
- OpenAI's speed claims and GitHub's early quality observations are vendor-reported and need workload-specific validation.

### Why it matters specifically to Jet — interpretation
- P2's proposed **AI Workflow Proof Sprint** already measures accepted-result rate, intervention, completion time, and cost per accepted result. These launches make **deadline-weighted routing** commercially testable: cheap/fast models for interactive or high-volume steps; premium high-speed inference only where waiting creates measurable staff or customer cost.
- This supports a sharper offer position than “we choose the best AI model”: **Limitless proves which workflow steps deserve speed, which deserve quality, and which should be routed to lower-cost capacity.**

- Commercialize the result as a paid remediation: a client-specific **Model Routing Policy** with three lanes—economy, standard, urgent—and explicit escalation thresholds. Do not build a new dashboard; reuse the existing Proof Sprint and Agent Morning Command Center assets.
