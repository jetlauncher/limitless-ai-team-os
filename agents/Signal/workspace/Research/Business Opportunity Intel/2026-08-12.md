## 1. Model routing is becoming a measurable AI-cost control—not just an engineering trick

- **Verified sources/date:** NVIDIA’s official launch — **August 11, 2026**; GitHub’s official Copilot changelog — **August 11, 2026**.[1][2]
- **What changed — fact:** NVIDIA released the open Nemotron 3.5 Lightning model and open-source NeMo Switchyard router. Switchyard can route each workflow step across open and proprietary models by quality, latency, or cost without rewriting the application. NVIDIA reports internal results near one-third the cost of using Opus 4.8 alone and partner reductions of 27–74%; these are vendor/partner-reported benchmarks, not independent audits. GitHub separately rolled out MAI-Code-1.1-Flash with vision support at a stated **73% lower list price** than MAI-Code-1-Flash and a 0.25× Copilot premium-request multiplier.[1][2]
- **Why Jet should care — interpretation:** Yesterday’s Sonnet 5 price signal established a cheaper frontier baseline; today’s releases show the next savings layer is **routing simple work away from frontier models**. That creates a defensible audit metric: clients should not be judged on “which model they use,” but on cost per accepted result by task class.
- **Revenue/offer action:** If P2 is activated, add a **routing test** to its 10-task rubric: run each task with the default frontier model and a cheaper specialized route, then compare accepted-result rate, runtime, and total cost. Sell the remediation as a quantified cost-control upgrade—not a generic “use a cheaper model” recommendation.
