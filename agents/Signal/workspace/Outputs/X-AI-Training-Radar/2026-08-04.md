### 1. Google DeepMind's SkillSmith — Inference-Time Skill Composition
**The signal:** DeepMind published "SkillSmith: Learning to Compose Parametric Skills and Textual Knowledge" (arXiv 2607.27497). A frozen Gemma 3 4B model composes new capabilities at inference time by combining existing K-V caches with text descriptions — no retraining needed.

**Key numbers:** Composite-SNI benchmark reached Elo 1,455 (K-V only), 1,622 (text-only), **1,714 (combined)**. This is significantly better than either alone.

**Why it matters for AI training/agents:** Skill composition becomes an inference-time operation, not a new training run. For agents, this means you could theoretically give them new capabilities on-the-fly without retraining — imagine handing a business AI OS a new SOP and having it immediately adapt its behavior.

**Source:** [Daily.dev coverage](https://daily.dev/posts/google-deepmind-s-skillsmith-composes-model-skills-at-inference-time-by-treating-weights-as-input-lnci2xijp) · [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27497)
**→ Best Jet angle:** Content about "instant AI adaptation" — a post showing how inference-time skill composition makes the dream of plug-and-play AI skills actually feasible. Could tie to his content pillars: teach business owners that AI doesn't always need retraining — sometimes you just compose what it already knows.

### 3. Test-Time Training + World Modeling — AI That Learens While It Works
**The signal (Yejin Choi @ NVIDIA):** End-to-end Test-Time Training where an LLM continuously updates its weights while reading through long context at test time — not just prompting, but actual weight-updating during inference. Paper: ["Learning to Discover at Test Time"](https://test-time-training.github.io/discover/).

**Signal (Cameron Wolfe @x) - World Modeling for Agents:** After reviewing recent papers on world modeling for language agents, the main finding: incorporating a world modeling objective into agent training improves performance — fewer interaction turns, fewer tool calls, fewer output tokens to solve problems. The LLM is trained to predict tool outputs using SFT.

**Why it matters:** Both point toward AI that doesn't just "process" data but actively learns from it in real time. For business operators: this means your AI agents will get better the more they work with your specific data — without manual retraining. The learning curve starts at deployment, not after it.

**Source:** [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/reimagining-llm-memory-using-context-as-training-data-unlocks-models-that-learn-at-test-time/) · [Yejin Choi on X](https://test-time-training.github.io/discover/) · [CWolfeResearch on world modeling](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2082159833625788591)
**→ Best Jet angle:** "Your AI agents will get better automatically just by using them" — this is the future of Team OS. Content about building systems that compound intelligence through daily use, not one-time setup."
