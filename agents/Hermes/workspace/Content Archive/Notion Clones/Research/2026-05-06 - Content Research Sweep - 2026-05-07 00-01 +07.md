## **Top 3 Angles**

### **1. Customer feedback is becoming an operating system, not a survey folder**

Why it matters: Founders can now turn messy comments, support tickets, NPS, sales notes, and app reviews into prioritized product, marketing, and service decisions. The opportunity is not ‘summarize feedback’; it is closing the loop from signal → decision → owner → shipped improvement.

Recommended formats: Thai authority post; LinkedIn operator memo; carousel: ‘Customer Feedback → Operating Intelligence’; reel teardown of a weekly feedback loop.

Hook ideas: Your customers are already writing your product roadmap. AI just makes the signal visible. / Stop asking AI to summarize feedback. Ask it to assign the next operating decision. / The best AI workflow for many businesses is hiding in their complaint inbox.

- AWS/Hapag-Lloyd signal: customer-facing digital teams used Bedrock, Elasticsearch, LangChain, and LangGraph to transform feedback into actionable insights.

- Intercom’s swarm/product-signal framing reinforces that least-scalable customer work often contains the most scalable product insight.

- Founder move: define a weekly Customer Signal Review with categories, severity, revenue impact, owner, and next action.

Source signal: AWS ML Blog; Intercom Blog

### **2. Agent quality decays after launch — treat AI like a product, not a plugin**

Why it matters: A working agent is not finished. Models change, users change, prompts get reused, edge cases accumulate, and quality quietly drifts. Founder leverage comes from traces, evaluation sets, A/B tests, rollback rules, and improvement cadence.

Recommended formats: Carousel: ‘The Agent QA Loop’; Thai post on why AI pilots fail after week two; LinkedIn checklist; workshop lesson for Limitless members.

Hook ideas: Your AI agent does not fail on launch day. It fails silently two weeks later. / If your agent has no evaluation loop, it is not automation. It is a liability. / Most AI pilots die because nobody owns quality after the demo.

- AWS AgentCore Optimization explicitly frames production traces, batch evaluation, A/B testing, and quality degradation as the core lifecycle problem.

- OpenAI B2B Signals emphasizes adoption systems and agentic workflows, not isolated tool usage.

- Founder move: give each agent an owner, metric, eval set, exception log, and monthly review.

Source signal: AWS ML Blog; OpenAI RSS

### **3. AI dashboards are turning into question engines**

Why it matters: Dashboards answer known questions. Operators usually need the next unknown question: why did revenue dip, which segment changed, what should we inspect next? Natural-language BI shifts data work from waiting for reports to interrogating the business in real time.

Recommended formats: Thai founder post; carousel: ‘Dashboard Era vs Question Era’; LinkedIn story; short reel using a CFO/marketing example.

Hook ideas: Dashboards tell you what happened. AI lets you ask why before the meeting ends. / The next BI skill is not building charts. It is asking better operating questions. / If your team waits days for a new dashboard, your decision loop is too slow for the AI era.

- AWS Quick Dataset Q&A and dashboard generation signals show structured data becoming conversational and multi-dataset.

- Google’s ‘turn your data into decisions’ framing reinforces data simplification and growth-signal clarity.

- Founder move: build a weekly ‘questions backlog’ for sales, finance, operations, and marketing — then turn repeat questions into live dashboards.

Source signal: AWS ML Blog; Google Keyword RSS

### **5. Agents are getting hands — now identity and permissions become the product**

Why it matters: Once agents can control browsers, native UIs, mouse/keyboard, files, and external services, capability is no longer the main question. The question is who the agent is allowed to be, what it can touch, and how the business audits the action.

Recommended formats: Carousel: ‘Agent Permissions Ladder’; Thai post; founder memo; ops SOP template.

Hook ideas: The scary part is not that agents can think. It is that agents can click. / Before your agent gets hands, give it an ID badge. / AI automation without permissions is just risk at machine speed.

- AWS AgentCore Browser OS Level Actions exposes screenshot + mouse/keyboard control beyond web-layer APIs.

- AWS AgentCore Identity on ECS highlights OAuth, scoped tokens, session binding, and secure access for production agents.

- Founder move: separate read-only, draft, approve, and execute permissions; log every external action.
