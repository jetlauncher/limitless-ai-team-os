## Top 3 Angles

### 1. Agent PRs are the new management surface

Why it matters: AI coding is moving from “help me write code” to agents producing work that needs review, cost control, and trust gates. Founders who treat agent output like normal human PRs will miss hidden debt, hallucinated intent, and runaway API spend.

Recommended formats: LinkedIn post, Thai authority post, founder checklist carousel, engineering leadership reel

Hook ideas:

- Your next employee may open 30 pull requests a day. Who reviews them?

- AI coding is no longer a writing problem. It is a review-system problem.

- The bottleneck is not code generation — it is code acceptance.

Supporting points:

- GitHub highlights practical review patterns for agent-generated PRs: look for hidden assumptions, technical debt, brittle tests, and changes that are correct locally but wrong strategically.

- GitHub also flagged token efficiency in production agentic workflows — always-on agents can quietly accumulate large API bills.

- This pairs with OpenAI’s Codex safety framing: sandboxing, approvals, network policy, telemetry, and compliance are now part of the AI coding stack.

Source signal: GitHub Blog AI RSS: “Agent pull requests are everywhere. Here’s how to review them”; “Improving token efficiency in GitHub Agentic Workflows”; OpenAI RSS: “Running Codex safely at OpenAI.”

### 2. AI search is turning source strategy into a distribution moat

Why it matters: Google’s Preferred Sources debate signals a bigger shift: founders can no longer assume open-web SEO alone will create discovery. In AI search, being trusted, cited, selected, or excluded becomes a strategic growth lever.

Recommended formats: LinkedIn thought piece, Thai founder post, carousel: “The new SEO is source strategy”, newsletter segment

Hook ideas:

- The new SEO question is not “Can Google find you?” It is “Will AI trust you?”

- AI search is making distribution less democratic — and more strategic.

- Your content moat is becoming a source moat.

Supporting points:

- The Decoder argues Google’s Preferred Sources shifts responsibility to users while Google’s AI interfaces continue reshaping traffic.

- For founders, this implies content must be structured as citeable, source-worthy, expert-owned material — not just keyword pages.

- Practical content angle: build owned insights, founder POV, comparison pages, case studies, and data pages that AI systems can safely quote.

Source signal: The Decoder RSS: “Google’s Preferred Sources feature…”; Google Blog/AI RSS around AI Search/AI Mode surfaces.

### 3. Token efficiency becomes an operating KPI

Why it matters: As agentic workflows run on every PR, ticket, call, or customer event, AI cost stops being a subscription line item and becomes operational leakage. Teams need dashboards for agent spend per outcome, not just model bills.

Recommended formats: Founder ops post, tactical carousel, short reel: “AI automation has hidden COGS”, workshop module

Hook ideas:

- Your AI agent may be doing the work — and quietly burning the margin.

- The next AI dashboard is not usage. It is cost per useful action.

- Automating a bad loop just creates expensive motion.

Supporting points:

- GitHub’s production workflow note frames token efficiency as an engineering problem that can be instrumented and optimized.

- AWS GPU capacity signals show infrastructure availability/planning is now part of AI launch readiness.

- Tie to founder metric: cost per resolved ticket, qualified lead, merged PR, generated insight, or completed workflow.

Source signal: GitHub Blog AI RSS: token efficiency in agentic workflows; AWS ML Blog: EC2 Capacity Blocks for ML and SageMaker training plans.

### 7. AI evaluation is moving from vibes to verifiable rewards

Why it matters: AWS’s verifiable-rewards RL post and GitHub’s nondeterministic agent validation point to a more mature AI operations pattern: judge agents by objective outcomes, constraints, and repeatable checks — not just impressive demos.

Recommended formats: Founder education post, carousel, course lesson on AI QA, LinkedIn technical-but-business post

Hook ideas:

- If you cannot verify the output, you cannot scale the agent.

- AI quality is not a prompt. It is a measurement system.

- The founder question is changing from “Does it work?” to “How do we know?”

Supporting points:

- Verifiable rewards work best where correctness can be objectively checked: math, code, symbolic tasks, structured workflows.

- For fuzzy business tasks, define proxies: acceptance rate, escalation rate, customer satisfaction, revenue attribution, human override rate.

- This is a strong bridge from technical AI news to founder operating discipline.

Source signal: AWS ML Blog: verifiable rewards/GRPO on SageMaker AI; GitHub Blog AI RSS: validating agentic behavior when correct is not deterministic.
