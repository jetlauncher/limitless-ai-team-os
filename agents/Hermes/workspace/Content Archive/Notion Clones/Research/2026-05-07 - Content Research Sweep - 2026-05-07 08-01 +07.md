# Top 3 Angles

## 1. Frontier companies are turning AI adoption into an operating advantage — not a tool rollout

- Why it matters: Founders need a clearer AI maturity ladder. OpenAI’s B2B Signals framing points to deeper adoption, Codex-powered agentic workflows, and durable advantage; the teachable point is that the winners redesign work and management cadence, not just procurement.

- Recommended formats: LinkedIn authority post, Thai founder carousel, workshop lesson, founder scorecard checklist

- Hook ideas: The AI gap is no longer access. It is operating maturity. / Two companies can use the same model. Only one turns it into a compounding advantage. / Stop asking “which AI tool?” Ask: “which workflow now runs better every week?”

- Supporting point: AI advantage now shows up in depth of adoption: more workflows, clearer owners, better context, and tighter review loops.

- Supporting point: Codex/agentic workflows are becoming a pattern for recurring work rather than one-off prompting.

- Supporting point: Founder takeaway: create an AI operating cadence — weekly workflow selection, KPI baseline, agent deployment, QA review, improvement backlog.

- Source signal: OpenAI News RSS: “How frontier enterprises are building an AI advantage” (May 6, 2026) — frontier enterprises deepen AI adoption, scale Codex-powered agentic workflows, and build durable competitive advantage.

## 2. Agent quality is becoming a continuous management function

- Why it matters: Production agents will drift: prompts get reused, models change, customers behave differently, and edge cases accumulate. Founders should treat agent performance like sales conversion or product quality — measured, reviewed, A/B tested, and improved.

- Recommended formats: Carousel: “Your agent needs a manager”, LinkedIn tactical post, ops SOP, course module on AI QA

- Hook ideas: Your AI agent will get worse unless someone manages it. / Prompting is the launch. Evaluation is the business system. / The hidden AI role every company needs: agent quality manager.

- Supporting point: AWS AgentCore Quality Optimization focuses on production traces, batch evaluation, A/B testing, and recommendations.

- Supporting point: MLflow/SageMaker signals reinforce observability and evaluation as normal generative-AI development practice.

- Supporting point: Founder framework: collect traces → label failures → test fixes → A/B changes → review weekly.

- Source signal: AWS ML Blog: “Introducing agent quality optimization in AgentCore, now in preview” (May 4, 2026); “Streamlining generative AI development with MLflow v3.10…” (May 5, 2026).

## 3. Agent identity is the new company security perimeter

- Why it matters: As agents access CRMs, files, browsers, email, finance systems, and customer data, the biggest risk is no longer only hallucination — it is permissioning. Founders need to explain AI security as scoped access, session binding, recovery, auditability, and account protection.

- Recommended formats: Founder warning post, Thai carousel, checklist, short reel on “AI security before AI scale”

- Hook ideas: Your AI agent is not a chatbot anymore. It is an employee with keys. / Before you scale agents, decide what they are allowed to touch. / The next AI mistake won’t be a bad answer. It will be an over-permissioned agent.

- Supporting point: AgentCore Identity examples include OAuth, secure session binding, scoped tokens, ECS/EKS/Lambda/on-prem deployment.

- Supporting point: OpenAI’s Advanced Account Security signal reinforces stronger login and recovery around sensitive data.

- Supporting point: Founder action: inventory agent tools, define permission scopes, separate approval steps, log every high-risk action.

- Source signal: AWS ML Blog: “Secure AI agents with Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Identity on Amazon ECS” (May 5, 2026); OpenAI News RSS: “Introducing Advanced Account Security” (Apr 30, 2026).
