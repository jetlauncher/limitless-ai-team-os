## Top 3 Angles

### 1. Voice agents are becoming customer-service products, not IVR upgrades

- Why it matters: Parloa/OpenAI’s fresh signal makes voice AI feel less like a demo and more like a deployable service layer: simulate conversations, control reliability, and serve customers in real time.

- Recommended formats: Thai authority post, 45–60s reel, carousel: “Voice AI is not a bot. It is your new front desk.”

- Hook ideas: The next customer-service hire may not type. It will talk. / Most companies will fail at voice AI because they treat it like a chatbot with a microphone. / Voice agents are where bad processes become painfully obvious.

- Supporting points: Voice adds latency, interruptions, emotion, and trust pressure — the workflow design bar is higher than text chat. Founders should map call types, handoff moments, tone rules, escalation triggers, and unacceptable answers before buying voice AI. Offer angle: “AI Voice Workflow Audit” for service teams: call-map, scripts, escalation rules, measurement dashboard.

- Source signal: OpenAI: Parloa builds scalable, voice-driven AI customer service agents.

### 2. Customer acquisition is shifting from ad placement to assistant placement

- Why it matters: ChatGPT self-serve ads, Google AI Search exploration, and personal-agent races all point to the same market shift: customers will increasingly discover through AI-mediated surfaces.

- Recommended formats: Thai authority post, LinkedIn POV, carousel: “SEO → AEO → Agent Placement”

- Hook ideas: Your next buyer may ask an AI before they ever see your website. / The new marketing question: would an assistant recommend you? / ChatGPT ads are not just ads. They are a preview of agent-mediated demand.

- Supporting points: Founders must make offers machine-readable: clear category, proof, price logic, exclusions, FAQs, comparisons, and trust signals. Ad buying may become less about keywords and more about training signals, structured data, creative truthfulness, and recommendation eligibility. Repeated theme from recent sweeps, but the newly fresh layer is SMB access to ChatGPT ads and Google’s AI Search exploration UX.

- Source signal: OpenAI ads RSS + Google AI Search updates + The Decoder report on self-serve ChatGPT ads/personal agents.

### 3. Production agents need identity before autonomy

- Why it matters: AWS AgentCore Identity and OS-level actions show agents are being allowed to touch real systems. For founders, permissions, audit logs, and accountability become prerequisites.

- Recommended formats: LinkedIn post, operator checklist, premium carousel

- Hook ideas: Never give an AI agent more access than you can explain in one sentence. / The future of AI ops is not autonomy. It is accountable autonomy. / Before your agent clicks, ask: who is it acting as?

- Supporting points: Every agent needs role, scope, credentials, allowed systems, forbidden actions, audit trail, human approval points. OS-level/browser actions increase leverage but also increase blast radius. This is a founder-friendly governance angle: “small teams need enterprise-grade permissions earlier than they think.”

- Source signal: AWS: AgentCore Identity on ECS; AgentCore Browser OS Level Actions.

### 6. AI unit economics are becoming product strategy

- Why it matters: AWS Inferentia2 vision-language deployment and massive reported compute deals point to a practical truth: the winning AI product is not only smarter — it is cheaper enough to run at scale.

- Recommended formats: Founder memo, carousel: “The hidden AI moat: cost per useful action,” LinkedIn post

- Hook ideas: Your AI product can be impressive and still economically impossible. / Cost per useful action is the new SaaS gross margin. / The model choice is a pricing decision, not just a technical decision.

- Supporting points: Founders should track cost per resolved ticket, qualified lead, generated report, reviewed document, or completed action. Inference optimization, smaller models, fallback routing, and task segmentation become business-model choices. Use this to teach why “best model” is not always “best product.”

- Source signal: AWS pet-tech VLM deployment on Inferentia2 + The Decoder reports on major compute commitments.
