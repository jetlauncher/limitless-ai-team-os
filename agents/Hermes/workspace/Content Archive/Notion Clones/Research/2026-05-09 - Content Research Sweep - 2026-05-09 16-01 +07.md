## Top 3 Angles

### 1. Bad AI measurement will become the new management theater

- Why it matters: Emotion AI in the workplace is a warning signal for founders: automating a weak proxy does not create truth. If the metric is pseudoscientific, biased, or disconnected from business outcomes, AI only makes bad management faster and more scalable.

- Recommended formats: Thai authority post; LinkedIn contrarian memo; carousel: ‘AI metrics that will ruin your company’; founder workshop lesson on choosing AI scoreboards.

- Hook ideas: The most dangerous AI in your company may not hallucinate — it may measure the wrong thing perfectly. / AI can make bad management look scientific. / Before you automate a metric, ask whether the metric deserves to exist.

- Supporting points: The Decoder reported an Atlantic story on emotion AI entering workplaces, while recent sweeps also surfaced fakeable reasoning traces. Together they point to one founder lesson: do not confuse machine-readable signals with reliable judgment.

- Source signal: The Decoder RSS, May 9 and May 8, 2026.

### 2. Trusted access is becoming the enterprise AI moat

- Why it matters: AI systems are now touching security research, code, customer data, bank workflows, and internal tools. The blocker is not model capability alone — it is who gets access, under what constraints, with what logging, and how risky actions are approved.

- Recommended formats: Thai founder warning post; LinkedIn memo; carousel: ‘Before AI touches your business’; internal AI governance checklist.

- Hook ideas: The next AI moat is not a smarter prompt. It is trusted access. / Your agent is only as safe as its permissions. / AI adoption is becoming an identity and access-management problem.

- Supporting points: OpenAI is expanding trusted access for cyber models and publishes Codex safety practices; AWS has AgentCore Identity and OS-level browser actions; Anthropic packages agents for financial services. All point to a production-AI reality: permissions and auditability are now product features.

- Source signal: OpenAI RSS; AWS ML Blog; Anthropic News, May 2026.

### 3. AI agents are crossing the money line

- Why it matters: Once agents can transact, qualify leads, guide shoppers, or trigger payments, founders need to redesign unit economics and approvals. ‘AI automation’ becomes a financial workflow, not just a productivity feature.

- Recommended formats: LinkedIn thread; Thai carousel: ‘When agents start spending money’; founder finance/ops lesson; offer angle for AI payment-risk audit.

- Hook ideas: The moment your AI can pay, it stops being a chatbot. / If an agent can create revenue, it can also create financial risk. / Outcome-priced AI will force founders to know what each workflow is worth.

- Supporting points: AWS introduced AgentCore Payments with Coinbase and Stripe; Intercom is building outcome-based pricing for Fin for Sales; Fin for Ecommerce moves customer agents closer to checkout. The pattern: AI is moving from advice to economic events.

- Source signal: AWS ML Blog May 7; Intercom Blog May 7–8, 2026.

- Hook ideas: Stop asking whether voice AI sounds human. Ask whether it finishes the job. / Your next form might be a conversation that actually resolves the workflow. / Voice AI becomes valuable when it removes operational friction, not when it wins a Turing test.

- Supporting points: OpenAI announced new voice intelligence models; Parloa builds service agents; ElevenLabs highlights local/on-prem/on-device voice AI plus support proof points like Klarna/Revolut. The fresh framing is workflow completion and privacy-fit deployment.
