## Top 3 Angles

### 1. Safe agents are becoming SOPs with logs, approvals, and permissions — not just smarter assistants.

- Why it matters: OpenAI’s Codex safety framing gives founders a usable blueprint for every AI workflow: isolate the work, limit network/data access, require approval for risky actions, and log what happened. This moves AI adoption from trust-the-tool to design-the-operating-system.

- Recommended formats: Thai authority post; carousel: ‘Before you let AI do work, give it rails’; team training checklist; LinkedIn operator memo.

- Hooks: ถ้าคุณให้ AI แตะลูกค้า เงิน หรือ code แต่ไม่มี log ไม่มี approval ไม่มีสิทธิ์ชัดเจน — ปัญหาไม่ได้อยู่ที่ AI แล้วครับ / The question is no longer ‘Is the agent smart?’ It is ‘What can it touch, who approves it, and what gets logged?’ / Your first AI policy should look less like a legal document and more like an SOP.

- Supporting points: OpenAI RSS describes Codex safety with sandboxing, approvals, network policies, and agent-native telemetry. This pattern translates beyond coding: sales follow-ups, invoices, customer refunds, ad budget changes, and data exports all need the same rails. Founder move: choose one workflow and write four rules — allowed data, forbidden actions, approval trigger, log owner.

- Source signal: OpenAI — Running Codex safely at OpenAI (RSS-derived signal).

- Jet personalization: Strongest Jet fit: systems over superheroes + Thai owners scaling without headcount. Use a Thai owner example where AI can draft quotations or customer replies, but discounts/refunds/payment promises still need approval.

### 2. AI makes ad production cheaper; the scarce skill is now the founder’s brief.

- Why it matters: Google’s Small Brief signal is valuable because it reframes AI creative for SMEs: the machine can produce, remix, and polish faster, but only if the owner can express the customer, offer, pain, proof, and constraint clearly.

- Recommended formats: Thai reel; carousel: ‘Your AI ad is only as good as your brief’; founder workshop lesson; Limitless content-to-revenue post.

- Hooks: AI ไม่ได้ทำให้ทุกคนมี ads ดีขึ้น — มันทำให้คนที่ brief ชัดชนะเร็วขึ้น / The bottleneck in AI advertising is not Canva, Veo, or Gemini. It is whether the founder can explain why customers should care. / A weak brief becomes 30 weak creatives faster.

- Supporting points: Google launched The Small Brief, using ad legends + AI to make ads for local small businesses. This is not a ‘cool AI creative’ angle; it is an operator angle about brief quality, customer insight, proof, and offer clarity. Monday move: write a one-page brief before generating any ad — customer, pain, promise, proof, CTA, forbidden claims.

- Source signal: Google Blog RSS — The Small Brief (May 8, 2026).

- Jet personalization: Tie to Jet’s wisdom-in-noise theme: Thai owners do not need more content output; they need clearer business judgment encoded into the brief.

### 3. AI media buying is becoming budget governance, not button-click optimization.

- Why it matters: Google’s AI-powered bidding and budgeting updates point to a founder-level shift: marketing teams must define goals, margins, budget constraints, conversion quality, and feedback loops because the system will act faster than manual operators can inspect every move.

- Recommended formats: Founder memo; Thai carousel: ‘ก่อนเพิ่มงบ Ads ให้ audit 5 เรื่องนี้’; sales/revenue workflow post; marketing team SOP.

- Hooks: The algorithm cannot fix an unclear offer. It can only spend money distributing the confusion. / In AI ads, your media buyer is only as good as your margin, conversion data, and constraint design. / ก่อนเพิ่มงบโฆษณา ถามก่อนว่า AI กำลัง optimize เพื่อยอดขายจริง หรือแค่ตัวเลขที่ดูดีใน dashboard

- Supporting points: Google Ads/Commerce RSS frames AI bidding and budgeting around goals, budget control, and shifting consumer behavior. Founder implication: define contribution margin, qualified lead, refund risk, cash cycle, and offline sales feedback before trusting automated budgets. Connect AI ads to sales team reality: if LINE replies die, faster bidding just buys more leaked revenue.

- Source signal: Google Blog RSS — AI-powered bidding and budgeting innovations in Search and Shopping (May 7, 2026).

- Jet personalization: Connect to balance-sheet discipline and sales/revenue workflows: Thai owners should ask whether AI is improving profit and cash conversion, not just lowering CPL.

### 5. Answer-engine ads will force founders to earn trust before they buy attention.

- Why it matters: OpenAI testing ads in ChatGPT matters because paid discovery is entering answer environments where users expect help, not interruption. The founder implication is offer trust, claim discipline, privacy language, and proof become more important than raw ad volume.

- Hook ideas: When ads enter AI answers, trust becomes the placement. / If your offer cannot survive a skeptical AI answer, more budget will not save it. / The next ad channel may punish vague claims faster than Facebook ever did.

- Supporting points: OpenAI RSS says ChatGPT ad tests include clear labeling, answer independence, privacy protections, and user control. This is early, but commercially important: AI answer surfaces may blend discovery, advice, and paid placement under stricter trust expectations. Founder move: clean up claims, proof, customer fit, privacy language, and post-click handoff.

- Source signal: OpenAI RSS — Testing ads in ChatGPT (May 7, 2026).

- Jet personalization note: Make it Thai-owner specific: before chasing a new ad channel, make the offer explainable, truthful, and operationally deliverable.

### 6. Event-driven AI is the hidden operator upgrade: stop waiting, start designing handoffs.

- Why it matters: Gemini API webhooks are a technical signal with a non-technical founder implication: long-running AI work should trigger the next business step automatically — review, customer notification, task creation, invoice drafting — instead of people checking dashboards manually.

- Recommended content formats: Operator post; automation lesson; carousel: ‘Polling vs workflow’; internal SOP example.

- Hook ideas: The most boring AI update may save your team the most time: stop checking, start triggering. / AI workflows fail when humans have to remember to check if the job finished. / If your team is still refreshing dashboards, you have not built a workflow yet.

- Supporting points: Google AI RSS describes webhooks for long-running Gemini API jobs to reduce polling friction and latency. Translate to Thai SMEs: lead enrichment completes → salesperson gets LINE task; document extraction completes → accountant reviews; customer summary completes → CRM updates. This is a systems-over-memory angle: don’t leave the next step inside someone’s head.

- Source signal: Google AI RSS — Gemini API webhooks (May 4, 2026).

- Jet personalization note: Very Jet: ‘business cannot scale on memory in a few people’s heads.’ Show one SOP where AI completion triggers the next owner automatically.

### 7. Powerful AI access is becoming role-based, verified, and purpose-bound.

- Why it matters: OpenAI’s Trusted Access for Cyber signal is broader than cybersecurity: the market is separating casual AI use from high-risk capabilities that require verified users, permissions, and monitoring. Founders should mirror this inside their own companies.

- Recommended content formats: Thai authority post; leadership memo; team policy checklist.

- Hook ideas: Not everyone in your company should have the same AI access. / AI-first leadership is not giving every team every tool. It is matching access to responsibility. / The more powerful the AI, the more boring your permission system must become.

- Supporting points: OpenAI RSS frames GPT-5.5-Cyber access around verified defenders and critical infrastructure protection. Internal business parallel: finance data, customer exports, discount approvals, HR docs, and ad budgets should have different AI access levels. Founder move: define three tiers — personal productivity, team workflow, sensitive business action.

- Source signal: OpenAI RSS — Scaling Trusted Access for Cyber with GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.5-Cyber (May 7, 2026).

- Jet personalization note: Connect to AI-first leader and training teams: good leaders don’t just encourage AI use; they design access by role and risk.
