## Top 3 Angles

### 1. Your AI agent now needs a permission system, not just a prompt

- Why it matters: As agents move from answering to acting on computers, code, accounts, and data, the operational risk shifts from “bad answer” to “bad action.” Founders need approvals, logs, network limits, and kill switches before scaling agents into real work.

- Recommended formats: Thai authority post, carousel, founder checklist, operator workshop lesson

- Hook ideas: ถ้า AI ของคุณกดปุ่มแทนคนได้ SOP ของคุณต้องกลายเป็น security system แล้วครับ / The next AI mistake won’t be a wrong answer. It will be a wrong action with access. / Before giving an agent a workflow, decide what it is never allowed to touch.

- Supporting points: OpenAI’s Codex safety framing emphasizes sandboxing, approvals, network policy, and telemetry.; The Decoder reports agent hacking/copying capability is improving quickly; use this conservatively as a risk signal, not a claim to overhype.; For Thai SMEs, the practical boundary is: customer data, ad spend, payment, discounting, refunds, invoice edits, payroll, and supplier communications.

- Source signal: OpenAI Codex safety RSS; The Decoder/Palisade and Palo Alto/METR reports as secondary signals.

- Jet personalization note: Connect to Jet’s systems-over-superheroes theme: “ถ้าให้ AI แตะงานขาย ข้อมูลลูกค้า หรือเงินบริษัท แต่ไม่มี log/test/approval ปัญหาไม่ได้อยู่ที่ AI แต่อยู่ที่ระบบของเราเอง.”

### 2. AI cost is becoming a gross-margin question, not a software-subscription question

- Why it matters: Model prices, GPU capacity, and chip supply commitments mean AI adoption can quietly become a margin leak if founders deploy it everywhere without usage rules, routing, and ROI thresholds.

- Recommended formats: LinkedIn/X thread, CFO-style Thai post, internal CEO memo, class module on AI P&L discipline

- Hook ideas: AI ที่แพงที่สุดไม่ใช่ตัวที่ subscription แพงที่สุด แต่คือตัวที่ทีมใช้โดยไม่มีหลักคิดเรื่อง margin / Your AI budget is no longer “tools.” It is compute, usage length, quality routing, and business margin. / Before scaling AI, ask: which workflow deserves the expensive model?

- Supporting points: The Decoder reports GPT-5.5 may cost materially more in real usage despite shorter-response claims.; Broadcom/OpenAI/Microsoft chip-demand reporting shows AI infrastructure depends on committed demand, not infinite cheap supply.; AWS capacity planning posts show even enterprises now reserve short-term GPU capacity; operators must plan compute like inventory/cashflow.

- Source signal: The Decoder model-cost and custom-chip reports; AWS EC2 Capacity Blocks/SageMaker training plans signal.

- Jet personalization note: Tie to balance-sheet discipline: Thai owners should map AI usage to revenue workflows — lead qualification, support deflection, quotation speed — before letting every team run expensive models for low-value tasks.

### 3. Outcome-priced AI will change how founders buy software

- Why it matters: Sales/support AI is moving away from generic seats toward measurable business outcomes: qualified leads, transactions, checkouts, and payments. That changes vendor selection, dashboards, and team incentives.

- Recommended formats: Thai founder post, sales-ops lesson, B2B offer angle, carousel “from seats to outcomes”

- Hook ideas: อีกไม่นานคำถามจะไม่ใช่ “AI ตัวนี้ราคาเท่าไหร่” แต่คือ “มันรับผิดชอบผลลัพธ์อะไร?” / When AI can qualify leads and take payments, software pricing starts to look like commission. / The best AI vendors will sell outcomes. The best founders will know which outcomes to measure.

- Supporting points: Intercom is discussing outcome-based pricing for Fin for Sales where value ties to qualified leads.; Fin for Ecommerce positions a customer agent from browsing to checkout.; AWS AgentCore Payments previews agents that transact via payment rails under business rules.

- Source signal: Intercom Fin for Sales/outcome pricing and Fin for Ecommerce RSS; AWS AgentCore Payments RSS.

- Jet personalization note: Make this Jet-specific through sales/revenue workflow: define what counts as a qualified lead, who approves discounts, what goes into CRM, and when AI hands off to a human sales closer.
